- **In the build.** Every stage has an acceptance check that stops the build and writes the failing rows. A manifest is written only when all of them pass.

## 4. Decisions you made

- **Python**, in `residual-attractor/`; GDSS's MATLAB is reference only.
- **Any series.** Nothing in the code depends on which series or which configurations a run holds.
- **The archive rule.** Every configuration's residuals for every series are derived and kept one by one. Nothing is fused, pooled or weighted across residuals before the archive is finished, and every later analysis acts on each retained residual on its own. Redundant configurations stay.
- **Failure scale.** σ × each configuration's own training-span growth; σ is the plain 40-step window.
- **Cadence.** The spec's nine labels, per ladder.
- **No A(t)**, because it averages across configurations.
- **Twins stay** (Holt with β = 0 is SES); they are marked from the registry and never tested against each other.
- **rdiag is reworked** for the rule (contract v2) and its analyses are rebuilt inside this package, per configuration.

## 5. What the archive shows so far

Views on build `171464ad5372997a`: standardized one-step residuals, 2018-01 to 2026-02. Nothing here is across series.

**On levels, the AR grid measures its own drift.** Run spec 0 reads every series in levels (`transform: level`). The AR configurations are stationary around a running mean, so on a trending level they forecast back toward a mean the series has left.

| | AR (29) | SES (9) | Holt (16) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residuals beyond 2σ (a calibrated normal: 4.6%) | 66.9% | 9.8% | 8.5% |
| Residuals beyond 4σ | 43.0% | 1.7% | 1.3% |
| Mean squared standardized residual | 59.7 | 2.2 | 1.8 |

- **Pair tests.** 12,127 of 19,978 tested pairs differ at p < 0.05, unadjusted. Among pairs with no AR member the share is 7.9%; with one AR member, 78.5%. The significant differences are AR against the rest.
- **Confidence sets.** On ten series the set keeps 17 to 24 of 50 models and drops nearly every AR configuration (0 to 3 of 29 survive). On four (PALUMUSDM, PNICKUSDM, BSCICP02USM460S, STLFSI4) it keeps 49 or 50: the tests cannot separate the configurations there.
- **σ runs low.** Even the best configuration's mean squared standardized residual is above 1 on every series (1.01 to 2.60). Holdout errors are larger than the trailing 40-step σ predicts.
- **Cadence.** 47.2% Holds, 40.1% Immediate, 9.1% Oscillating, 2.5% Insufficient; the other five labels share 1.1%.

**In first differences, nothing separates the configurations.** I rebuilt the same panel with `transform: diff` in a scratch folder (not committed; it is one changed line in the run spec).

- AR calibrates like the others: 8.2% of its residuals lie beyond 2σ, against 8.7% for SES and Holt.
- No pair differs at p < 0.05 (0 of 19,978; the smallest p is 0.055), and all 50 models stay in every series' confidence set.
- A few months carry the loss. Three months hold a median 42% of a configuration's total squared loss, and the worst month is most often April 2020 (190 of 756 configuration-series rows), then March 2023 and March 2022.

**What follows from that.**

- Stage 10 cannot yet say which configurations are informative: on levels it reports AR's drift, and in differences it reports nothing.
- The cross-series stages will meet the same months. April 2020 hits most series at once, so any co-movement finding has to say how much of it rests on that month.
- Clark-West has never run on real data, because no configuration takes another series as input. Predictive lead-lag evidence cannot exist until one does.
- Run spec 0 has not been compared with the spring outputs. The spring run paired targets with indicators, and nothing here does that yet.
